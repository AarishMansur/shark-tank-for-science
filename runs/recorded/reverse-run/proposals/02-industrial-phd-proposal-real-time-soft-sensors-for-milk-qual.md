This three-year industrial PhD project bridges raw milk intake profiling with bioreactor control to stop next-day production losses in dairy manufacturing. By mapping farm-to-farm variables like somatic cell counts, fat, protein, and temperature to fermentation kinetics, the initiative introduces real-time soft sensors for yoghurt and cheese lines. The research pairs the dairy processing operations in Vojvodina with advanced biochemical engineering expertise from the Faculty of Technology, University of Novi Sad.

## The problem
The dairy in Vojvodina runs yoghurt and cheese lines that suffer production losses because milk quality varies from farm to farm. Currently, these variations are only identified through lab tests the next day, preventing proactive intervention during processing.

## Research questions
1. How can intake variables such as temperature, somatic cell counts, fat, and protein readings be mathematically mapped to fermentation outcomes in real time?
2. Can soft sensors combining intake parameters with mid-infrared spectroscopy and pH-stat base consumption predict lactic acid fermentation shifts before downstream processing failures occur?
3. What adaptive control algorithms can stabilize product quality across varying raw milk compositions in pilot-scale and industrial stirred-tank bioreactors?
